1. The market prices about three more hikes than the Fed projects

Treasury yields are the market's forecast of short rates plus a small premium. The 1-year yield of 4.41% implies the Fed funds rate averages about 4.4% over the next twelve months, up from 3.88% today. The forward rate for the following year, read off the 1-year and 2-year yields, is 4.89%. That is the Fed holding at 4.75-5.00%, roughly four quarter-point hikes above today.

The Fed's September projections put the rate at 4.1% at the end of both 2026 and 2027: one more hike, then a hold. Reuters' poll of 100 economists (Oct 16-20) found all 100 expecting a hike on Dec 9, and about 60% expecting the peak at 4.00-4.25%.

The December hike is in both paths, so this trade does not depend on it. The bet is on 2027: the market's rate for that year is 0.86 points above the Fed's.

2. The 2-year pays the most per unit of risk

Each step out the curve adds a little yield and a lot of price risk. The last column is how far yields can rise over a year before each fund's extra income is gone. SHY can absorb a 0.49-point rise. IEF absorbs 0.16 and TLT 0.08, and the 10-year moved 0.17 today alone.

Yield is the iShares average yield to maturity on Oct 21, moved by each fund's segment of the Treasury curve on Oct 22. Roll-down is the gain from bonds aging into lower-yielding maturities on today's curve: about 0.30 points a year for SHY, 0.23 for IEF and slightly negative for TLT, because 20-year yields sit above 30-year yields.

Long bonds carry a second risk the 2-year mostly avoids

A long yield is the expected path of short rates plus a term premium, the extra investors demand to hold a long bond. The San Francisco Fed puts the 10-year term premium at 1.35 points, up from 1.04 a year ago. Treasury announces auction sizes on Nov 4. Since August it has said coupon auction sizes stay unchanged "for at least the next several quarters." Dropping that line would push long yields up even if the Fed stops hiking. The 2-year moves almost entirely on the Fed path, which is where the mispricing is.

Long bonds hedged stocks today: TLT gained about 1.8% while the Nasdaq fell 2.6%. If your portfolio is mostly S&P 500 and you want that hedge, that is a separate decision. This brief is about where to hold cash.

How returns are calculated

Bond fund return ≈ yield − fee + roll-down − duration × change in yield. Bills return the average bill yield over the year minus the fee. SHY uses the 2-year yield change, IEF the 10-year and TLT the 30-year. The model leaves out convexity, which adds to returns when yields move a lot in either direction: about half a point for TLT in scenarios 3 to 5, less for IEF and nothing that matters for SHY. Returns are before taxes and trading costs.

4. The case against, and the answer to each point

Inflation is speeding up, so the market is right to price more hikes.

Core CPI rose at a 3.4% annual rate over the last three months, against 2.6% over the past year, and September producer prices posted their biggest increase in years. This is the strongest objection. It is why the oil-shock scenario gets 15% and why the exit sits at 5.05%. Core PCE, the measure the Fed targets, rose 0.2% in August.

The Fed's projections are stale.

They were made Sep 16, before the hot September CPI. New ones come with the Dec 9 decision. If the median for end-2027 rises to 4.6% or more, the Fed has moved to the market's path and the reason for the trade is gone: sell SHY.

Chair Warsh is a hawk.

The September hike passed 12-0, and his Oct 16 speech talked about "synchronous tightening." The market already prices four hikes from here. SHY loses to bills only if the Fed delivers more than that.

Oil could spike if the US strikes Iran.

Brent hit about $113 today, its highest since May, on reports of an operation planned for after the Nov 3 midterms. Higher oil lifts headline inflation within a month but also cuts consumers' real spending, and the Fed sets policy on core inflation. Brent above $125 is a reason to cut the position in half.

The labor market is fine.

Layoffs are low: jobless claims fell to 194,000 this week and unemployment is 4.2%. Hiring is weak: payrolls grew 41,000 a month over the past year and 29,000 in September. Low layoffs with slow hiring argues for the Fed stopping, since it does not need to cool a labor market that is barely adding jobs.

6. How to place it

With a brokerage account

  1. Sell SGOV, or use cash already in the account.
  2. Buy SHY with a limit order at the current ask. The spread is usually one cent. Trade between 10:00am and 3:30pm ET, when spreads are tightest.
  3. SHY's NAV was $81.50 on Oct 22, so $500 buys 6 shares for about $489. Set dividends to reinvest; SHY pays monthly.
  4. Set price alerts on the 2-year yield at 5.05% and 4.15%, or check the live line at the top of this page.

Costs and taxes

SHY's expense ratio is 0.15% a year, already counted in the numbers above. Treasury interest is exempt from state and local income tax and taxed federally as ordinary income.

Direct alternative

Treasury auctions 2-year notes every month, usually in the last week. Buying one at auction through TreasuryDirect ($100 minimum) or a broker locks in the yield to maturity with no fund fee. Selling before maturity on TreasuryDirect requires moving the note to a broker first, so use the fund if you might exit early.

Record the entry, then grade it

Entry on Oct 22, 2026: SHY NAV $81.50, SGOV NAV $100.64, 2-year yield 4.65%, 1-year forward rate 4.89%. On Dec 10 and on Oct 22, 2027, compare SHY's total return with SGOV's and write down which scenario actually happened. If SHY wins for a reason outside the scenarios, count it as a miss on the analysis.

Terms

Treasury bill, note, bond
Loans to the US government. Bills mature within a year, notes in 2 to 10 years, bonds in 20 or 30.
Yield
The annual return from holding a bond to maturity at today's price. When yields rise, existing bonds fall in price.
Duration
Price sensitivity. A duration of 1.79 means a 1-point rise in yields cuts the price about 1.79%.
Forward rate
The rate the curve implies for a future period. If a 1-year note yields 4.41% and a 2-year 4.65%, rolling 1-year notes only matches the 2-year if next year's 1-year rate is 4.89%.
Term premium
The extra yield investors demand for holding a long bond instead of rolling short ones. It cannot be observed directly; the SF Fed estimates it with a model.
Roll-down
When the curve slopes up, a bond gains value as it ages into a shorter, lower-yielding maturity.
SGOV, SHY, IEF, TLT
iShares Treasury funds holding 0-3 month bills, 1-3 year notes, 7-10 year notes and 20+ year bonds.
