Bills, 2-year, 10-year or 30-year Treasuries for the next 12 months

Four iShares funds cover the choice: SGOV holds 0-3 month bills, SHY 1-3 year notes, IEF 7-10 year notes and TLT 20+ year bonds. Over a year, the winner depends on where the Fed and long-term yields end up compared with what prices already assume. Section 4 turns your own odds on six scenarios into a pick.

1. What the market prices and what the Fed projects

Treasury yields imply a path for the Fed funds rate. The 1-year yield of 4.41% implies a 4.4% average Fed funds rate over the next twelve months, against 3.88% today. The rate for the following year, read off the 1-year and 2-year yields, is 4.89%. That matches the Fed holding at 4.75-5.00% after four more quarter-point hikes.

The Fed's September projections put the rate at 4.1% at the end of both 2026 and 2027, one hike above today. Reuters polled 100 economists on Oct 16-20. All 100 expect a hike on Dec 9, and about 60% expect the peak at 4.00-4.25%.

The two paths agree on December and split on 2027, where the market's forward rate sits 0.86 points above the Fed's projection. The Fed's numbers date from Sep 16. Since then September CPI rose 0.6%, producer prices posted their biggest increase in years and Brent climbed to $113. Market prices include that news, and the Fed publishes new projections on Dec 9.

Energy is pushing headline inflation well above core. Gasoline made up more than half of September's 0.6% CPI rise. Core is the part the Fed responds to, and it is speeding up: 3.4% annualized over three months against 2.6% over twelve. At 0.3% a month, core runs near 3.6% a year, which gives the Fed reason to keep hiking past December. Core PCE, the Fed's target measure, rose a slower 0.2% in August. The 10-year breakeven of 2.33% says bond investors expect inflation to average 2.3% over the next decade, well below today's 3.7%, so they treat the current pickup as temporary.

A long yield is the expected path of short rates plus a term premium, the extra investors demand for holding a long bond. The San Francisco Fed estimates the 10-year term premium at 1.35 points, up 0.31 in a year. Over the same year its expected-rate component rose 0.78 points, so most of the rise in long yields came from the expected Fed path. A high term premium means long bonds pay more for their risk today. A further rise pushes their prices down even if the Fed stops. Treasury announces auction sizes on Nov 4, and since August it has said coupon auction sizes stay unchanged "for at least the next several quarters." Dropping that line would point to more long-bond supply. The 2-year depends mostly on the Fed path and very little on the term premium.

Oct 22 showed both shocks at once. Doubts about AI spending sank tech stocks, and Meta fell nearly 8% after its earnings and a new share class to fund AI. Oil rose on reports that the US and Israel are weighing strikes on Iran after the Nov 3 midterms. Treasuries rallied as a haven, and the 10-year had its largest one-day drop since March 2023. Long bonds hedge stocks when the worry is growth. When the worry is inflation, as in 2022, stocks and long bonds fall together. An oil shock does both: it raises headline inflation within a month and cuts consumers' real spending over the following quarters.

An AI boom hurts long bonds through real yields. If AI delivers a real jump in productivity, investors expect faster growth and a higher neutral interest rate, and companies borrow heavily to build. Both push long real yields up. Chow, Halperin and Mazlish argue that a market expecting transformative AI would push long real rates up sharply, and across 59 countries they find higher long-run growth expectations go with higher long-term real rates. In 1999, during the last productivity boom, the 10-year yield rose from 4.7% to 6.4% and the Fed hiked from 4.75% to 5.50% while stocks rallied. Long bonds gain in an AI bust and lose in an AI boom. Stocks do the reverse.

How returns are calculated

Bond fund return ≈ yield − fee + roll-down − duration × change in yield. Bills return the average bill yield over the year minus the fee. SHY uses the 2-year yield change, IEF the 10-year and TLT the 30-year. The model leaves out convexity, which adds to returns when yields move a lot in either direction. That is about half a point for TLT in scenarios 3 to 6, less for IEF and nothing that matters for SHY. Returns are before taxes and trading costs.

Placing the trade

  1. Use a limit order at the current ask. Spreads on these funds are usually one cent.
  2. Trade between 10:00am and 3:30pm ET, when spreads are tightest.
  3. Set dividends to reinvest. All four pay monthly.
  4. Treasury interest is exempt from state and local income tax and taxed federally as ordinary income.

Buying a note directly

Treasury auctions 2-, 10- and 30-year securities every month. Buying at auction through TreasuryDirect ($100 minimum) or a broker locks in the yield to maturity with no fund fee. Selling before maturity from TreasuryDirect means moving the security to a broker first, so a fund is simpler if you may exit early.

Grading it

On the review date, compare your fund's total return with SGOV's over the same period and write down which scenario actually happened. A call that made money for a reason outside your scenarios counts as a miss on the analysis.

Terms

Treasury bill, note, bond
Loans to the US government. Bills mature within a year, notes in 2 to 10 years, bonds in 20 or 30.
Yield
The annual return from holding a bond to maturity at today's price. When yields rise, existing bonds fall in price.
Duration
Price sensitivity. A duration of 1.79 means a 1-point rise in yields cuts the price about 1.79%.
Forward rate
The rate the curve implies for a future period. With a 1-year note at 4.41% and a 2-year at 4.65%, rolling 1-year notes matches the 2-year only if next year's 1-year rate is 4.89%.
Term premium
The extra yield investors demand for holding a long bond instead of rolling short ones. Nobody observes it directly; the SF Fed estimates it with a model.
Roll-down
On an upward-sloping curve, a bond gains value as it ages into a shorter, lower-yielding maturity.
Breakeven inflation
The gap between a regular Treasury yield and an inflation-protected one. It approximates the inflation rate bond investors expect, plus a premium for inflation risk.
SGOV, SHY, IEF, TLT
iShares Treasury funds holding 0-3 month bills, 1-3 year notes, 7-10 year notes and 20+ year bonds.
